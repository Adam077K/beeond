### 1. `landing-page/L21-qintara-hero-black-voxel-flower.jpg`

**Founder:** "The hero visual direction, experience, and overall feeling: a combination of ASCII-art layout, motion, and something simple. The flower could be replaced with a half-bee, half-robot character. This is an example with a black background — I really like it."

**What's in the frame:** A full-bleed pure-black hero for "qintara". A dark pill-shaped nav bar floats centred at the top (Services · How it works · AI Security · Integrations · Resources) with the lowercase wordmark far left and a white "Get started" pill far right. Two forearms enter from the left and right edges with index fingers pointing inward — the left a silver-grey robot hand, the right a warm skin-toned human hand — both reproduced as a regular square grid of circular dots that thins to nothing toward the page edges. Between the fingertips sits a small isometric cluster of extruded violet voxel blocks arranged as a six-armed star, with a white-hot core, orange-red bloom and thin white flare rays; a row of five tiny monochrome pixel-drawn technical icons sits at the bottom centre. Centred over the gap: a two-line white grotesk headline, a two-line grey monospace subhead set with wide letterspacing, and a white pill CTA "See it in action →". The eye lands on the violet glow first, then climbs to the headline.

**Match check:** `PARTIAL` — three things differ. (a) This file is **pixel-identical** to `L11-qintara-dotted-hands-black-purple.jpg`, already in the index; verified by pixel diff (zero differing pixels). (b) The centrepiece is an isometric voxel star/snowflake built from cube blocks, not a flower — nothing botanical is in the frame. (c) The rendering is a circular dot-matrix halftone on a square grid, not ASCII glyphs; only the monospace subhead and the pixel icon row read as ASCII. Motion cannot be judged from a still. Black background, "something simple", and a swappable central character all confirm.

**Operative for us:** Two dot-screened hands enter from opposite edges; the one glowing object between them is the hero.

## What repeats across all seven

1. **The art is the layout.** In six of the seven, one image system occupies a full-bleed band and the type is set over or under it. None of them use decorative garnish beside the copy.
2. **Colour is rationed against a near-monochrome ground.** Orange only on a CTA (L12), colour only inside cards (B19), one accent gradient in a field of cream (L13), one violet glow on black (L21).
3. **Two hands reaching toward a point of contact** appears in three of seven — L21, L12 and the closing band of L13 — always as the human-meets-machine moment.
4. **One grotesk plus one other voice.** A script serif dropped mid-headline (L10), a light serif against bold grotesk (A01), a monospace subhead under a grotesk headline (L21), serif headings over grotesk body (L13).

## Note on the word "ASCII"

The founder uses "ASCII" across four of these notes for what are in fact four different techniques:
a glyph grid of plus signs (L10), a circular dot-matrix on a square grid (L21), irregular pointillist
stipple (L12), and — in A01 — nothing of the kind at all. Read his "ASCII" as *an image rebuilt from a
repeating small mark* rather than as literal character art, and settle the specific technique separately.
