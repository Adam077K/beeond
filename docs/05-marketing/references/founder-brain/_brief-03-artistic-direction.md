# Part 3 — Intended artistic direction

The founder gave one statement covering all seven references rather than per-file notes:

> "The artistic direction I want is grounded in real-world details: people, objects, places, landscapes, and color. These real and human visuals should be combined with an additional layer — either subtle or dramatic — of ASCII, pixels, typography, or other effects. The goal is to create a distinctive balance between **life and the real world** and **technology**."

Below, each of the seven files is described from the image itself. The closing block turns the seven into mechanism.

---

### 1. `B17-ascii-wind-turbine-editorial-climate-ai.jpg`

**What's in the frame:** A photograph of a single wind turbine shot from below against a deep cobalt sky, with a strip of olive-and-rust scrubland along the bottom edge and a second turbine small on the right horizon; the whole photo carries visible film grain and faint vertical scratches. Laid over it in white monospace characters is a **second turbine in a different rotor position** — a vertical mast and a wide horizontal blade-pair, drawn in ASCII, hung on the same hub as the photographed one so the two rotations read as one machine caught in two frames at once. The mark set is narrow and used as a weight ramp: `#` marks the contours and the leading edges, `.` fills the interiors as long dotted runs, and `+` and `-` appear as single transitional marks where an edge softens. The character grid has generous tracking and leading, so the rows read as discrete lines rather than a solid mass, and the photograph is fully visible through every gap — the ASCII is purely additive white, knocking nothing out. Editorial type sits separately: white sans words — *Could / AI / Actually / Offer / Climate / Solutions?* — scattered across the open sky at alternating left-right positions so the sentence assembles as the eye travels, plus a small serif line and a wordmark centred at the very bottom. No type ever crosses the subject.

**Real ↔ tech balance:** `SUBTLE-to-MID`. The photograph is untouched and dominant; the ASCII is a transparent second exposure occupying roughly a third of the frame.

**Palette:** saturated cobalt sky darkening at the top corners and paling toward the horizon; bone-white blades with two cadmium-red safety bands; olive-khaki and rust-brown scrub; white type.

**Operative for us:** ASCII redraws the same subject in a second position, superimposed — nothing is hidden.

### 2. `B20-ascii-pixel-overlay-blurred-flower.jpg`

**What's in the frame:** A photograph of red flowers against a navy background, thrown so far out of focus that only colour masses survive — crimson blooms, an indigo field, a warm tan plane bottom-left. Centred on it, a **flower-shaped mass of white monospace glyphs**, hard-clipped to a stepped, staircase silhouette so that the effect exists only inside the shape and the photo outside it is left entirely alone. The mark set is much wider than B17's: `#`, `*`, `%`, `x`, `e`, `=`, `:`, `.`, `-`, `+`, set on a strict grid with even character pitch and baseline, each glyph carrying a soft white bloom. Woven through the glyph field is a second system — stepped rounded rectangles outlined in a thin white-pink hairline and filled with the photo colour beneath them, reading like selection marquees or UI cells dropped into the type. Under the glyphs the photograph is pushed harder than it is outside: deeper crimson, blacker blacks, so the contrast between white mark and coloured ground is manufactured, not inherited.

**Real ↔ tech balance:** `DRAMATIC`. Inside the silhouette the glyph field is the subject and the flower survives only as colour; outside, the photo is untouched. The frame holds both extremes at once.

**Palette:** navy-indigo, crimson and magenta-red, dusty rose, warm tan, small blacks; glyphs pure white.

**Operative for us:** Clip a dense glyph field to the subject's silhouette; blur and intensify everything outside it.

### 3. `A21-eye-closeup-pixel-glitch-iridescent.jpg`

**What's in the frame:** An extreme macro of a human eye — hazel-green iris with visible fibre radiating, black pupil, lashes, brow hair, and skin pores all sharp. Two technological layers sit on it. First, a **fine regular mesh across the entire frame**, a woven screen texture at low contrast, strongest in the smooth passages at the top corners and lower half; it never varies and never follows the subject. Second, a **burst of small horizontal rectangular marks** — short bars and dashes of varying width, arranged in strict horizontal rows like corrupted scanlines or unreadable data — concentrated in a mass centred on the pupil. The burst behaves differently above and below: across the upper lid it runs as long horizontal strata that trail off left in ladders, and below the pupil it fans outward in radial streaks that follow the iris fibres and the lower lashes. The marks invert against their ground (pale on the dark iris, dark on the bright sclera), so they read as sampled from the photo rather than pasted onto it. The pupil itself is left completely clean — a pure black void at the exact centre of the disturbance. Diffuse prismatic bloom arcs top-left and glitters bottom-right. *Unclear:* whether the mesh is a rephotographed screen or a synthetic overlay.

**Real ↔ tech balance:** `DRAMATIC` in treatment, yet the eye reads instantly — the effect is loud but structurally obedient to the anatomy underneath.

**Palette:** oyster grey-lavender overall cast; peach and tan skin, sienna brow; hazel-olive iris ringed amber; black pupil; the glitch is silver-white shot with lilac, mint, rose and pale gold.

**Operative for us:** Key the disruption to the subject's own geometry — radial at the focal point, stratified along its edges — and leave the focal point itself clean.

### 4. `B05-halftone-dot-blue-sky-clouds-texture.jpg`

**What's in the frame:** Cumulus clouds on a blue sky, with no photograph surviving as photograph — the whole image is rebuilt as an **AM halftone on a square, orthogonal grid** (rows and columns at 0°/90°, not the printer's 45°), at one constant pitch across the entire frame. The only variable is dot radius, driven by luminance: tiny white dots in the open sky, growing through the cloud edges until neighbouring dots touch, then merge. Past 50% coverage the mechanism inverts in the classic way — the blue becomes the mark, surviving as small four-pointed stars at the cell corners, which themselves shrink to blue pinpricks and finally vanish into solid white at the cloud cores. So the image has three tonal states, not a continuous ramp: empty ground, dot, and merged solid. The background blue is a smooth flat gradient carrying no grid of its own; the dots sit on it. There is no photographic grain, no texture, no imperfection anywhere.

**Real ↔ tech balance:** `MID`. The conversion is total and nothing photographic remains, yet the subject reads as sky at a glance. Of the seven this is the most decorative and the least tied to a specific subject — it works as ground, not as statement.

**Palette:** two-stop cobalt-to-azure gradient, white, and a pale grey-blue used for the midtone dots.

**Operative for us:** Constant grid pitch, dot radius carries the tone; let cores merge to solid and let the ground invert into the mark.

### 5. `B01-dithered-dot-pixel-album-cover-portrait.jpg`

**What's in the frame:** A square cover laid out as a diptych on warm paper-white, with a single grid pitch running across both halves and **opposite polarity on each side**. Left: a portrait rebuilt as circular dots of *constant diameter* on a square grid, where each dot is filled with a colour sampled straight from the source photo — rust, charcoal, olive, teal, warm grey, pale ice-blue, some dots split two-tone where the sampled cell straddled an edge. Tone is carried by occupancy, not size: cells below threshold are simply omitted and the paper shows through, so the face emerges out of voids and never resolves fully. Right: an untreated photograph — a figure in an oversized pale-blue shirt, dark hair and beard, sunglasses, under blown-out fluorescent light — with **solid white squares** on the same pitch punched over it, massed at the top and eroding downward into scattered single squares. One grid, read twice: additive colour on the left, subtractive white on the right. Type is deliberately quiet — tiny all-caps grotesque credits at the top-left and repeated bottom-left, dwarfed by the white space.

**Real ↔ tech balance:** Left panel `DRAMATIC` (image entirely replaced), right panel `SUBTLE` (photo intact under a thinning veil). The composition's whole idea is holding both ends of the scale side by side.

**Palette:** warm paper white, pale ice-blue garment, near-black hair, terracotta and rust, cool slate and teal, sage, mid grey.

**Operative for us:** One grid, two polarities — colour-sampled dots rebuild the image, white squares erode it.

### 6. `C02-ascii-dot-horse-overlay-photo.jpg`

**What's in the frame:** A soft, grainy, slightly degraded photograph of two horses running through snow at dusk — a dun horse in front, a chestnut behind, a black treeline, hazy lavender mountains, a wooden fence, and heavy motion blur on the legs. Over it, a **third horse drawn in warm cream glyphs**, mid-gallop in its own pose, positioned to overlap both photographed animals without registering to either. The mark set is `#`, `.`, `+`, `-` with `#` run in long horizontal blocks where the figure is densest and single dots where it is faintest; the marks carry a soft glow. Density is where all the modelling happens: the contour and barrel are near-solid runs, and the mane, tail and hoof extremities dissolve into isolated scattered marks with no hard boundary. The glyph runs lengthen over the darkest passages of the photo beneath, so the overlay is at least partly sampling what it sits on. The photo is fully visible through it — additive again, knocking nothing out.

**Real ↔ tech balance:** `MID`. The photograph stays completely legible, but the glyph horse is the loudest thing in the frame — carried as much by the warm-cream-on-cold-blue opposition as by the technique.

**Palette:** periwinkle and slate-blue snow, near-black forest, hazy lavender-grey mountains, dun and chestnut horses, one small red mark on the fence, cream-ivory glyphs.

**Operative for us:** Draw a ghost subject in warm marks over a cold photo — dense at the contour, scattering to nothing at the extremities.

### 7. `C08-ascii-letter-bird-silhouette-water.jpg`

**What's in the frame:** A photograph of dark rippled marsh water in horizontal bands, with ochre reeds at the lower corners, left completely untouched. Sitting in it, an egret in flight, cut out from its background and lifted to a high-key near-white — not flattened, because the wing's feather ridges and the body's soft grey shading are still visible underneath. Across that white bird runs a field of **black uppercase letters** on a monospace grid: real characters (A, B, C, K, Q, T, W, X, Y, Z) forming gibberish strings, with conspicuous long runs of a single repeated letter — `FAAAAAAAAABO`, `ZAAAAADEIMAABX` — so the field reads as machine output, not language. Letter density does the tonal work: cells are perhaps a third occupied across the bright wing, tightening along the leading edge and the top contour, and packing almost solid down the two legs, which are rendered as letters and nothing else. A faint yellow-green chromatic fringe traces the bird's back edge. One colour survives the conversion — the beak stays warm amber.

**Real ↔ tech balance:** `DRAMATIC` inside the silhouette, `zero` outside it. The split is purely spatial: the effect stops dead at the cutout edge.

**Palette:** deep slate-teal water with near-black troughs, olive-ochre reeds, paper-white bird, black letters, one warm amber beak.

**Operative for us:** Knock the subject out to near-white, fill it with letters whose density draws its own shadows, leave the background photo alone.

## The technique, made executable

- **The grid pitch is always constant. The per-cell value is what varies.** Not one of the seven scales, rotates or warps its grid. Modulation happens in exactly three ways, and they are interchangeable: **radius** (B05 — dot size tracks luminance), **occupancy** (B01-left, C08, C02 — the cell is either marked or empty and the ground shows through), and **weight along a character ramp** (B17, B20, C02 — `.` → `-` / `+` / `:` → `#` / `%` / `*`). Pick one per surface; do not mix radius and ramp in the same field.

- **Three combination methods, and each frame commits to one.** (a) *Ghost twin* — redraw the same subject in a second pose or rotation and lay it over the photo additively, offset and overlapping (B17, C02). (b) *Silhouette clip* — confine the effect to the subject's outline, with the photo outside untouched (B20, C08). (c) *Total conversion* — rebuild the whole surface as grid, keeping nothing photographic but sampled colour (B05, B01-left). A fourth, rarer: *anatomy-keyed disruption* — concentrate the effect on a focal point and propagate it along the subject's own directions (A21).

- **The effect is additive or subtractive, never a blend mode over the top.** Marks are opaque white/cream sitting on the photo (B17, B20, C02), opaque white squares punched out of it (B01-right), or black letters on a lifted white subject (C08). Nothing is set to multiply, overlay or 30% opacity. The photo reads at full strength through the gaps between marks — the gaps are the mechanism, not transparency.

- **Every field dissolves at its edge instead of stopping.** Density thins into isolated single marks at the extremities — mane and tail (C02), blade tips (B17), the bottom of the dither (B01-right), the face's edges (B01-left). The only permitted hard boundary is a deliberate silhouette clip (B20's staircase edge, C08's cutout), and those are stepped to the grid, never smooth vector curves.

- **The mark is always monochrome — one flat white, cream, or black. Colour never enters the glyph.** Colour survives in three places instead: showing through the gaps between marks (B17, B20, C02), sampled into the cell fill where the mark *is* the pixel (B01-left), or as a single untouched accent left deliberately intact — C08's amber beak, B17's red blade bands, C02's red fence mark. Preserve one warm accent per frame.

- **Warm mark on cold photo, or white mark on saturated photo.** The contrast doing the work is temperature, not just value: cream glyphs on periwinkle snow (C02), white on cobalt (B17, B05), white on crimson (B20). Where the underlying photo is too flat to carry it, push the photo's saturation and blacks *under* the mark field, as B20 does inside its silhouette while leaving the outside soft.

- **Keep one clean anchor per frame.** A21 leaves the pupil pure black at the centre of the disturbance; C08 leaves the entire background photograph untouched; B01 leaves half the cover as a straight photo; B05 leaves the open sky as flat gradient. The eye needs one uneffected place to land — without it, the treatment reads as a filter.

- **Sharp mark against soft, imperfect photograph.** Grain and scratches (B17), heavy bokeh (B20), motion blur and scan degradation (C02), skin pores and lens bloom (A21), blown flash (B01). The technological layer is razor-crisp and the photographic layer is emphatically not. This opposition is doing as much work as the grid itself — a clean stock photo under a clean grid produces B05, which is the weakest of the seven precisely because nothing in it is imperfect.

- **The distinguishing test between the ones that land and gimmick: does the tech layer have a job?** In B17 it re-times the rotor. In C02 it is a third animal. In C08 it draws the bird's actual shadow structure. In A21 it obeys the lids and the iris fibres. In B01 it samples the photo's real colours. B05 is the outlier — an evenly-applied conversion with no relationship to its subject beyond tone, which is why it functions as background texture and not as a statement. **Rule: the effect must be derived from the subject, not applied to it.** If the same overlay could be dropped on any other photo unchanged, it is decoration.

- **The type stays quiet while the image is loud.** Where typography appears alongside the treatment (B17, B01) it is small, plain grotesque, unstyled, set in negative space, and never crosses the subject — B17's headline is broken into single words scattered across empty sky at alternating positions so the sentence assembles as the eye travels; B01's credits are tiny and dwarfed by white space. The extravagance is spent entirely in the image. Note this is separate from typography used *as* the effect (C08's letter field, B20's glyph mass), where the characters are marks and deliberately spell nothing.
