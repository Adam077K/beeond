## Dreamy / premium direction

### 1. `B22-dreams-logo-cloud-shape-brand-match.jpg`

**Founder:** "A dreamy, clean, premium feeling."

**What's in the frame:** Two rounded-corner panels side by side on a white ground, separated by one gutter roughly equal to the corner radius. Left panel: a plain photographic blue sky (mid cyan-blue at top grading paler toward the horizon, a few small soft cumulus at the bottom edge) carrying a white lockup — a four-petal pinwheel mark plus the wordmark "Dreams" in a clean geometric-leaning grotesque with a small superscript ™, set left-of-centre and vertically centred. Right panel: the *same* four-petal mark rebuilt as photoreal cumulus cloud — four fluffy lobes meeting at a pinched centre, floating in the same sky. No shadows, no gradients, no texture; the whole idea is one flat brand asset and its literal weather-made twin.

**Match check:** `CONFIRMED` — dreamy comes from the sky and the cloud-object; clean and premium come from the restraint (two panels, one colour, one typeface, one mark).

**Operative for us:** Render the brand mark once as flat vector, once as a real photographed object.

## Grid & layout mechanics

How files 6-8 actually work, at a level a build agent can implement from:

- **Two grid species, not one.** `A22` is a *bento*: a fixed 2-column block where the left cell spans both rows and the right column splits into two unequal cells (roughly 16:9 on top, closer to square below). `C09` is a *masonry marquee*: rows of tiles with a constant height per row and freely varying widths, with row boundaries intentionally unaligned between rows. `C18` is a *single content column* with full-bleed backgrounds behind it. Pick the species per section; don't blend them.

- **The gutter is the only separator.** In `A22` cards sit on flat white with no border and no shadow; in `C09` tiles sit on flat black with hairline (~4-6px) gutters and no border. Both rely entirely on ground-versus-tile contrast. Adding a stroke or a drop shadow to either would break the read.

- **Corner radius is one value, applied everywhere.** Every card in `A22`, every tile in `C09`, every panel in `C03` uses the same moderate radius regardless of cell size. The gutter in `A22` is roughly equal to that radius — radius and gutter are set from one number.

- **One job per cell.** `A22`'s three cells are exactly: a portrait with a quote, a single number, a call to action. Nothing shares a cell. Where imagery and type do share (the quote card, the stat card), the type anchors to a cell edge — top-centred eyebrow, bottom-left quote, bottom-band caption — never floated in the middle of the picture.

- **Colour arrives as a whole-cell fill.** The acid-lime CTA in `A22` is one saturated block among neutral neighbours; in `C09` every drop of colour comes from the thumbnails themselves and the chrome stays monochrome white-on-black. No accent-coloured borders, badges, or icon tints anywhere in these three.

- **Cropping is a deliberate device.** `C09`'s rows bleed past both frame edges and `C03`'s wordmark runs off both sides — the cut edge signals continuation and scale. Tiles are cropped by the viewport, not resized to fit it.

- **Hairlines expose the column grid.** `C18` draws faint full-height vertical rules at the content column's edges. They carry no content; they are the only visible structure on an otherwise empty hero, and they are what makes the emptiness read as composed rather than unfinished.

- **Premium comes from what is left out.** `C18`'s hero holds a headline, two small buttons, one short paragraph and a light source — the middle of the viewport is empty. The restraint is the effect: few elements, one type pairing (display serif plus mono eyebrow plus sans body), one light source, large unbroken ground. Density (`C09`) and emptiness (`C18`) both read premium; what they share is that neither adds decoration on top of content.

- **Sections change ground, not grid.** `C18` flips near-black to a silver gradient at the section break while the same column grid and the same headline treatment continue through. The background is what marks the chapter.

- **Logo walls overlay, they don't get their own band.** `C09` fades a black scrim up over the bottom of the gallery and sets the logos on it — four per row, wide even spacing, all monochrome white and optically size-matched rather than mechanically equal-width.
