## 0. What this is bound by, so nothing below relitigates it

**LOCKED — `DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md`.** Neutral system, photography owns colour, one accent on the primary CTA only, two per page, two tokens bound to chapter (`#1F4D3D` light chapters · `#57B295` dark chapters, never swapped). Instrument Serif (regular and italic, no bold) · Schibsted Grotesk · IBM Plex Mono. Alternating bone/black grounds, chapter by chapter; the column grid continues through. One radius, gutter ≈ radius; no borders, no drop shadows, no glass; depth from overlap and soft shadow. Photograph shape bound to the photograph's job; every photo edge is a mosaic dissolve. Seven signature moments, each once on the whole site. One pinned scroll set-piece, below the fold, ≤ 90 frames, ≤ 1.5 MB, treated through the site's own renderer. Attention: 1–3 LOUD per page, never adjacent, loud or wordy never both.

**LOCKED — the law.** *The effect must be derived from the subject, not applied to it. If the same overlay could be dropped on any other photo unchanged, it is decoration.*

**LOCKED — the storyboard.** Four pages, twenty-one sections and one beat, their order, their attention marks, their text (PROVISIONAL, the founder's to edit). Phase 2 binds one visual and one motion decision to each; it does not add sections, cut sections, or rewrite copy. Where a visual needs a different line, the line goes back to the storyboard as an edit.

**LOCKED — evidence discipline.** No numbers as claims, no testimonials, logos, personas, status statements, product words, dashboards, coined terms, buzzwords. Numbers as instrument texture only.

**LOCKED — who it is for.** Arrival states S1–S5, never demographics. The site does not know a visitor's age, industry or company size and must not design as if it did. What it knows is how warm he arrived, what he is weighing, and how ready he is — that is what every visual decision below is checked against.

**LOCKED — what failed last time.** "The whole feeling": structure and imagery both. Phase 1 rebuilt the structure with the founder in the loop. This document therefore decides the **photographic world first**, the moments second, the motion third — because a set of beautiful sections in three different worlds is exactly what did not land.

**Not reused.** Nothing from the deleted 2026-09-02 build: not its three visions, not its photographs, not its renders. Its structure is gone; its imagery is gone.

## 1. The world — one photographic world for the whole site

### 1.1 The proposal · PROVISIONAL

**Outside, late in the day.** Every photograph on the site is from one continuous world: real people outdoors, shot from low so that open sky is the whole background; wildflowers and meadow at eye level; hands; warm afternoon light that is unmistakably a real afternoon and not a studio. No desks, no laptops, no offices, no screens, no keyboards, no whiteboards, no "team at work." The world is the one the founder's corpus keeps returning to, and it is the one that makes the technological layer *mean* something when it arrives: marks against sky, marks against petals, marks beside a real hand.

### 1.2 Why this world, from the corpus rather than from taste

- **Sky is the ground, not the subject.** Six of the eight branding references sit on sky or a flat sky-blue field (`_brief-02` §What repeats). The Beeond mark's own applied mockups are white-on-blue and white-on-a-blue-gradient (`_index-logo` Part 3). Sky is already the brand's ground before a single site decision is made.
- **The low angle.** B21 (woman, yellow sweater, phone), B12 (two friends, phones), C21 (man on the green ladder) — shot from below, sky fills the frame, "joyful, human, tech-adjacent." This is how a person is photographed in this world.
- **Warm against blue.** B21, B07 (yellow daisies), B18 (motion-blurred flowers, grain), A20 (golden foliage): the corpus's recurring temperature is gold or coral against sky blue. The design language's own compositing rule says temperature does the work — *warm mark on cold photo, or white mark on saturated photo.*
- **Hands reaching toward a point of contact** appear in three of the founder's seven website picks (L21, L12, L13) and twice more in branding (B09, B15) — always as the human-meets-machine moment. The site has exactly one such moment and it is the hero (§5).
- **Sharp mark against imperfect photograph.** Grain, bokeh, blown highlights, motion blur. The clean stock photo under a clean grid is the weakest image in the whole set (B05). So the photography is deliberately imperfect: grain, real light, real skin.
- **Flowers with a technological layer** is the founder's own group heading (`_brief-04`), and *bee, flowers, ink, sky, human* is his vibe line verbatim.

### 1.3 What is never photographed · PROVISIONAL

Offices, desks, laptops, phones-as-product, screens of any kind, "collaboration" tableaux, city skylines, abstract 3D objects, stock handshakes, any person who is not a founder presented as if they were staff. And never a dashboard, a report, a chart, or a thing that looks like an interface — the storyboard's A5 rule extends to every image on the site.

### 1.4 Where colour comes from, and the one tension · RISK, stated once

The system is neutral; the photographs carry gold and blue; the accent is deep pine on light chapters and pine tint on dark ones, on the CTA only. **Pine was measured against the two grounds, not against a photograph.** Wherever an accent-filled button sits inside a hero frame, it must sit on *ground* (the black or bone that the photo dissolves into), never on the sky. §5 places it there by construction. V1's `craft` review measures it anyway.

### 1.5 The ground map · PROVISIONAL

The background marks the chapter; the grid continues. Home reads as: **black** (hero) → **bone** (mechanism, standard, shape, coverage) → **black** (the comparison and the ask beat) → **bone** (before you ask) → **the meadow** (close, a photograph running off three edges over bone) → **bone** footer. Approach: bone throughout, one ground change at the close. About: bone, one ground change at the close. The Ask: bone throughout, no ground change — the most concentrated page on the site stays one surface. Ground changes are hard cuts. No gradients between chapters, ever.

## 2. The technological layer has one job: it is the swarm

**PROVISIONAL, and the sentence everything else hangs on.** The glyph fields, the dot screens, the marks — they are not "a tech feel." They are *how the swarm is present in a real world.* Humans are never converted into marks. Sky, flowers and the swarm's own presence are what the marks draw. The layer appears where the swarm is in the story and nowhere else, which is how the through-line's "lightly, and only sometimes" becomes a rule rather than a mood.

**The mark supplies the geometry, so nothing is invented.** The locked mark is four hive cells around a hidden cross: a small rounded heptagon, two chamfered cells in point symmetry, and one cell rendered as parallel diagonal stripes — flight lines (`_index-logo` Part 2). From it:

| Mark element | What it becomes on the site |
|---|---|
| The diagonal stripes (flight lines, roughly 45°, bottom-left to top-right) | **The site's one direction of motion.** Every reveal, every dissolve front, every density wave travels along it. Nothing slides up. |
| The small heptagon cell | **The organic blob crop** for every human portrait — the "ink-blot" shape the photography invariants already require, derived from this cell rather than drawn freehand |
| The cross-shaped gutter | **The hairline column grid** — the same negative-space cross, exposed as full-height rules |
| The hexagonal cell family | **The honeycomb mesh**, moment 5, a physical object the camera shoots through |
| The mark itself, in dots (LOGO-07's halftone-dissolve variant) | Proof that the mark obeys the same physics as every image — it can be used as the loading state and the favicon fallback (OPEN, §11) |

## 5. The hero, in full — because it is the decision · PROVISIONAL

**The concept.** *The swarm becomes one bee, beside a real hand.* One photograph: a real person, shot from low against open afternoon sky, looking up, one hand raised and open — the gesture the corpus repeats (L21, L12, B09, B15, L13). The photograph is untouched: skin, light, grain, sky. Across the open sky, a field of small cream marks, sparse at the frame edges, drifting slowly; the field thickens toward the raised hand and, a fingertip's distance from it, resolves into the silhouette of a single bee — dense at the body, dissolving to isolated marks at the wingtips. It is the founder's "half-bee, half-robot character" from the qintara note, rendered the system's way: not a drawn character, a swarm of marks that has become one thing.

**Why it passes the law.** The layer has a job: it *is* the swarm, present in the real world, coordinated as one thing — which is H3 block one's sentence, seen before it is read. Drop this overlay on any other photo and it means nothing; here it means the company.

**Composition.** Photo runs off the top, right and bottom edges (a full-bleed statement needs no edge treatment there). It dissolves into true black toward the left — mosaic dissolve, tiles appearing along the diagonal front — and the headline, deck, button and risk-reversal line sit on that black, in the open, never crossing the person. Type stays quiet while the image is loud: the headline is large but plain, one italic word at most. The button is accent-filled in the dark-chapter token, on black, which is where it was measured.

**Mobile.** The photograph is art-directed as a second crop, not scaled: portrait, the person and the hand in the upper two-thirds, the sky above them carrying the swarm, dissolving downward into black where the words sit. The bee stays a fingertip from the hand at both crops, so the photography brief must shoot for both.

**Motion.** The field is a canvas at a low frame rate; per-cell occupancy changes slowly so the swarm breathes and the wingtips flicker; the body holds. Density waves travel along the mark's diagonal. It does not react to the cursor, does not parallax, does not follow scroll. The photograph is the largest contentful element and loads first; the canvas starts after it. Under reduced motion the swarm is a still image rendered once.

**Alternates, with the reason each loses.** (B) *Silhouette clip on flowers* — the person holds wildflowers and the flowers alone are redrawn in colour-sampled glyphs (A12), sky and person untouched. Beautiful; loses because the layer's job is weaker (why are the flowers the machine?) and it spends the bee, which the founder asked for, nowhere. (C) *The same concept on bone* — loses because true black is load-bearing: the ground showing through between marks is the mechanism, and the founder said of the black hero "I really like it."

## 6. The scroll set-piece — subject and narrative · PROVISIONAL

`DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md` settled the pipeline and the budget and left the content open: *what it shows, and which section it pins to.*

**Section: H5, The shape of it.** Below the fold, as the budget requires; the section is already a sequence, so the scrub is honest rather than decorative.

**Subject: a single flower opening.** A short clip, tight on one bud against sky, generated on the founder-decided method (Higgsfield), split to frames, each frame run through the dot renderer — square grid, constant pitch, dot radius from luminance, colour sampled from the flower so the only colour in the middle of Home comes from this one thing. Scrubbed by scroll, reversible. **Foundation** is the closed bud and the first movement, almost nothing visible — which is what the text says of that phase. **Output** is the petals separating. **Compounding** is fully open, and the last scroll goes into making it better rather than opening it further: the dot pitch holds, the values settle. The three labels surface one at a time in the left column at the scrub points where the flower earns them.

**Why this subject and not the others.** (a) *Ink dispersing in water* — "ink" is in the founder's vibe line and it would render exquisitely in dots; loses because ink disperses and fades, which is the opposite of compounding. (b) *A honeycomb being filled* — the literal hive; loses because it is a hair from mark-as-real-object, which the language lists as not used, and it illustrates the name rather than the offer. (c) The flower is the world of §1 in motion, it is the founder's *flowers with a technological layer* heading, and its narrative is the section's own sentence.

**Mobile and low power.** Same frames, tighter crop, fewer of them; on `prefers-reduced-motion` a single poster frame of the open flower, no canvas, no listener.

## 7. The motion system — five rules · PROVISIONAL

1. **One direction.** Everything that moves, moves along the mark's flight lines: bottom-left to top-right. Dissolve fronts, density waves, the reveal order of stepped blocks. Never a slide-up, never a scale-in, never a fade-and-rise.
2. **Dissolve, never slide.** Marks appear by density. Photographs appear by mosaic dissolve. Type appears by plain opacity. Elements do not travel.
3. **One thing at a time.** In any viewport, at most one thing is in motion: the hero swarm, or the set-piece, or a hover. Motion never competes with a headline (cognitive load).
4. **Scroll behaves like scroll.** No parallax, no scroll-jacking, no sticky sections — except the one pinned set-piece, below the fold (Jakob's law, the one deliberate exception).
5. **Fast out, slow in, and short.** Asymmetric easing; exits faster than entrances; nothing over the design language's own window. Hover fills complete well inside the Doherty threshold; the submitted-form dissolve is the longest interaction on the site and still short.

**And under reduced motion:** everything above becomes a still. The site must read complete with zero animation, which is also what the engineering floor requires.

## 8. Typography — how the three faces behave · PROVISIONAL

- **Instrument Serif** carries every headline, every FAQ question, the chapter heads on Approach, the founders' names. Regular only; emphasis is **one italic word per headline at most** — the corpus's "one grotesk plus one other voice" (A20, L10, L19), done with the face the language already owns. No bold exists, so scale does the shouting and there is a ceiling on how loud words can get, which suits a site where the image is loud.
- **Schibsted Grotesk** carries every sentence meant to be read: decks, blocks, answers, bios, the Approach chapters, near sixty-five characters.
- **IBM Plex Mono** carries everything that is an instrument rather than a sentence: eyebrows (uppercase, tracked), field labels, the trust line, the two written rules at A4 and the rule at H4, the footer sign-off, the section index and the local time. The struck-through buzzword line at H4 is set in the *grotesk* and struck; the rule that killed it is in mono. That contrast is the card's whole drama.
- **Headings balance; running text does not exceed its measure; uppercase is tracked.** Inherited.
- **The wordmark — OPEN.** The mark is locked; the wordmark's face is not recorded anywhere. B5's knockout needs one. Working assumption for the brainstorm only: lowercase, in the display serif. The founder decides.

## 9. Layout — two grid species, and which section gets which · PROVISIONAL

The corpus gives two species and a third default (`ART-DIRECTION-BRIEF` §5): **bento**, **masonry marquee**, and **single content column with full-bleed grounds behind it.** Picked per section, never blended.

- **Single column with full-bleed grounds** is the site's default and is what every Approach, About and Ask section uses. Hairline full-height rules expose the column grid on every chapter; they carry nothing.
- **The stepped sequence** at H3 is a single column with a diagonal indent — a layout invented from the mark, not from the corpus, and the one place the site's structure argues.
- **No bento anywhere.** A three-equal-card row is on the anti-slop list, and the storyboard capped every group at three or four for Miller's law, which a bento would restate as tiles. Coverage (H6) is columns of type, not cards.
- **No masonry.** It exists for galleries of work, and there is no work to show.
- **One radius, one gutter, one number.** Cards (H4, the form's fields) and the blob crops' bounding cells all use it.
- **Cropping is a device.** The hero and the two closes run off edges; the knockout wordmark is cut by both edges. Tiles are cropped by the viewport, never resized to fit it.

## 10. The instrument layer — numbers as texture, and the one trap · PROVISIONAL

The founder asked for numbers in the futuristic register; the storyboard bans numbers as claims. Both hold: numbers are **texture**, small, mono, never a fact about the company.

- **A running head**, top of every page: the section index (`01 / 09 · HOME`), advancing with scroll — the one thing that moves on scroll other than the set-piece, and a Zeigarnik cue that the page has a length.
- **A local-time stamp with a "(Scroll)" cue** in the corner of the hero's black ground (Form & Found, L04) — **the visitor's own local time, never a city.** A named city fixes geography, and language and market scope are OPEN. Coordinates are out for the same reason.
- **The chapter anchors** on Approach (`01 · 02 · 03`).
- **Nothing else.** No counters, no "pieces this month," no ticking volume, no percentages as decoration. If a number could be mistaken for a claim, it is one.

## 11. Interaction details · PROVISIONAL

- **Nav.** Plain, persistent, inherits the chapter's tokens (so on black it uses the dark accent token for the outlined button, on bone the light one). Two links and the button fit at every width — no hamburger, no drawer. On The Ask, the button scrolls to the form.
- **Buttons.** Filled: ground-on-accent, no gradient, no shadow, no icon, no arrow. Outlined: accent border and label, fills on hover with asymmetric easing. Focus ring in ink, visible, never a hairline. Targets at or above the stated minimum, measured.
- **Links in text.** One in-body route (H4 → Approach); it is a sentence with an underline, and the underline is the whole affordance. Never a button.
- **The form.** Fields on white surface at the system radius, tall, full width; placeholder is not the label; the trust line is beneath the button, mono. On submit the form dissolves tile by tile into the ground along the diagonal and the confirmation note appears in its place — what happens next, no turnaround (§0.3.1). Error states in ink text under the field, no red (the system has no red).
- **Hover on photographs.** None. Photographs are not interactive and do not pretend to be.
- **Loading.** The mark in its halftone-dissolve construction (LOGO-07) as the one loading device, if any is needed; the site should not need one.
- **Favicon — OPEN.** The logo index warns the stripe cell smears below roughly thirty pixels; a simplified lockup for favicon scale is a founder decision, not a Phase 2 invention.
- **404.** Bone ground, one serif line, the outlined button home. No joke, no illustration.

### 17.2 The survey layer — `A15` · `B16` · `B10`

**Seen.** A weather-contour line traced over a blurred meadow with temperature call-outs and a mono day-label; one thin white circle and a scale bar over an aerial photograph of children; a plotted-dot circle over blurred foliage. Flat line-work that ignores perspective and turns a candid photo into evidence. The design language's own reserve names it *conceptually the best fit for a transparency pitch* and rates it EASY.

**For Beeond — annotate, never decorate.** Thin ink line-work at hairline weight drawn over photographs as if the frame had been surveyed:
- **H5, the flower set-piece:** the three phase labels stop being captions and become **plotted call-outs** — a contour line across the bud, *Foundation* pinned to a point on it; as the scrub advances the line redraws across the parting petals and *Output* pins; fully open, *Compounding*. - **B1, the founders:** name and role pinned to each portrait as a survey call-out with a leader line, in mono, rather than set beneath as a caption.
- **Approach, as plates:** Speakeasy frames every section in a visible hairline grid with corner ticks, like plates in a technical manual. The Approach page — the document one person sends another — takes the same frame: corner ticks, plate numbers `01 · 02 · 03`, nothing else.

**Why it fits.** It is additive, opaque line-work with the photograph at full strength through the gaps, so it obeys the compositing rules; it is derived from the subject (it measures *this* flower, names *this* person); and it says *observational, precise* — which is the feeling the transparency promise needs and no glyph field gives. **Cost:** SVG paths and mono labels, art-directable per breakpoint, still under reduced motion. **Budget:** it takes the slot the unspent double-exposure was holding — adding one means removing one. **The one rule:** the call-outs carry **words**, never figures. A15 plots temperatures; Beeond cannot plot a number that could be mistaken for a claim.

### 17.3 Light from the edges — `L09` · `C18` · `B09`

**Seen.** Axial's hero is two dot fields entering from the left and right page edges, thinning to nothing toward an enormous empty centre, three tiny captions anchoring the corners and a "[Scroll to Explore]" cue.
