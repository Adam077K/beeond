## 1. The overall thing — one page

**What the founder is reaching for, in my words after looking.** A real world — sky, skin in low sun, hands, wildflowers, warm light that is unmistakably an actual afternoon — into which the machine arrives as a *mark with a job*. Not a robot, not an orb, not a dashboard, not a glow: a way of seeing. Dots, glyphs, measurement lines, a ticking clock. The register he keeps picking is a fashion or culture campaign, not a software page: one photograph at a scale most sites will not risk, one voice-shift in the type, colour rationed to a single button, and the rest left empty. "Expensive" in his corpus is what is left out; "futuristic" is a mark that could only sit on *this* photograph; "human" is the constant that the marks are never allowed to touch. The bee is the swarm coordinating into one thing — never a character.

**The four images that hold it most completely.**
- **B17, the turbine.** Grain, cobalt sky, the same machine drawn a second time in additive glyphs, a sentence scattered across open sky, one quiet serif line at the foot. Every rule of the language is already in this one frame, and it is the founder's own artistic-direction pick.
- **L21, qintara.** Two hands in dots on true black with one glowing thing between them. He said of it "I really like it", and it is the only hero in the set he described in full. The lesson is the *feeling* (black, hands, one object, motion, "something simple"), not the voxel star.
- **B21, the low-angle woman in yellow.** Sky as the whole ground, one warm garment, a real smile — "beautiful colours and sky, clean, minimal, human". It is also, on close inspection, a generated image (B-43): poreless skin, no grain. The instinct is right and the file is the warning.
- **A12, the tulips.** Two flowers rebuilt as coloured characters, sky left clean. The one image where the effect is *derived* so completely that the flower is legible only as text — the law at its clearest, in the founder's own "nature with technological layers" group.

C06 (skin, sun, three type elements on one baseline) is the honourable fifth, and it is the image the founder gave no note to — which is often the truest kind of pick.

**What the corpus keeps repeating.** Sky as the ground, not the subject (six of eight branding files; the mark's own mockups are white on blue). The low angle (B21, B12, C21). Hands reaching toward a point of contact, always as the human-meets-machine moment (L21, L12, L13's close, B09, B15 — five files). Warm on cold: gold or coral on cobalt (B18, B07, A20, B21). A sharp mark on an imperfect photograph — grain, bokeh, blur (B17, C02, B20, A21); the one clean photo under a clean grid, B05, is the weakest image in the set by the founder's own ranking. Art as layout (six of seven website picks). One grotesk plus one other voice (L10's script, A01's light serif, L21's mono, L13's serif heads). Colour rationed: orange exactly twice on L12, one violet glow on L21, lime once on A22. And a quieter repeat nobody wrote down: the *emptied centre* — B09 is three-quarters blue, L09 is two dot fields around nothing, C18 is a headline and a beam.

**Where the corpus contradicts itself, and which side the locked language took.**
1. *Dark versus light.* L21, L10, C18 on black; L12, L13, B19, L08 on cream and grey. The language took both, by chapter: alternating grounds, hard boundaries. Every concept below has to decide what colour its first screen is, and it is the single biggest fork between them.
2. *Glass and gradient.* A01's frosted cards, A03's "New Asset" pill, L13's iridescent ribbons, B04's oil-slick clouds — the founder wrote "premium" against all of them. The language took the other side (no glass, no mesh) and kept what he was actually responding to: layering and depth from overlap.
3. *"ASCII" is four techniques.* Glyph grid (L10), dot matrix (L21), irregular stipple (L12), and in A01 nothing at all. The language kept glyph and dot as moments 1 and 2 and **dropped stipple entirely** — L12, the founder's cleanest "feeling of AI agents" reference, has no moment. Concept 4's hand-drawn line is the nearest thing to it.
4. *Human present versus absent.* L10, L20, L21, C18 have no real person; B21, B12, C06, A01, L22 do. The language chose humanity constant. Two concepts below (2 and 6) put a hand on screen one rather than a face, and say what that costs.
5. *Dense grids versus emptiness.* C09's masonry, A22's bento, B10's moodboard against C18 and L09. The language took emptiness as default (no bento, no masonry). The founder's grid notes remain un-cashed on the site; the ranking below treats that as correct for a site with no work to show.
6. *The half-robot.* L21's silver robot hand in dots is what the founder called the "half-bee, half-robot character"; L25's mascot on sky is what it becomes if drawn. The language's answer — humans never converted, the machine side may be marks — is the right split, and L-30 (the bee is a density peak, never a character) is the guardrail every concept keeps.

**What "award-winning" means against this corpus — what would make a jury stop.** A photograph that is obviously real and imperfect at a scale that takes nerve. A technological layer that could only exist on that photograph (a jury can tell a filter from a derivation in under a second). Restraint that reads as confidence: one accent, twice; three faces; no logos; an empty centre; words never on the image. One voice-shift in the type, not three. Motion that is rare and deterministic — one breathing field, one pinned scrub — rather than everything easing in on scroll. And at least one structural idea that inverts a cliché the jury has seen a thousand times (a comparison table with the seller's row left empty; a hero where the type is quiet and the picture is loud).

**What would make it look like every other AI-agency site** (the negative set, and the bank's §7): a centred stack — logo tile, eyebrow, headline, deck, two buttons; a dark hero with fog and a glow; an italic serif word bolted onto a grotesk headline; a frosted pill above the fold; a tilted dashboard; a logo strip; iconed reassurances under the buttons; a robot or an illustrated cast; particle fog with no subject; "AI-powered" anywhere; people at laptops. Concept 5 sits closest to the first of these by construction and says so.

### Concept 1 — The hand and the swarm

**Lead idea.** **A-30** (#5): the swarm obeys the hand. *Many small things, coordinated into one, beside a person who stays a person.*

**The hero, conceived.**
- *Real thing photographed:* one person, shot from low against open late-afternoon sky with no horizon (A-02), entering from the right and cropped by that edge (C-06), one warm garment and nothing else warm (B-42), one hand raised and open along the flight-line diagonal, gaze on the point a hand-width past the fingertips (L-07), an ordinary reason to be up against sky just out of frame (C-32).
