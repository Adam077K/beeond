## 0. The concept in five lines

1. Everything the swarm does is on the record, drawn over the real thing.
2. One person from directly above, their shadow the second subject; one thin ink line, hand-irregular and flat on the picture plane, drawn around person and shadow together, passing behind the shadow's tip, with one **word** interrupting it and never a figure.
3. The line's job: it marks this person's footprint, the ground they actually cover including the part they cannot see, which is the noun the site's only ask uses. On another photograph the ring encloses nothing.
4. No sky on screen one. No glyph field anywhere. The survey line is the site's one permitted style, spent at H1, H5 and About's name pins; every other mark on this site is a rule, an ordinal or a tick.
5. Home spends three moments plus the reserve style: dots at H5 as an inset plate, blur at H10, blueprint at H4. The dial at the hero is subtle.

**The italic (C-34).** Instrument Serif has no bold, so display emphasis is scale and one italic word, and the italic falls on the phrase that could be argued with, never the noun. Four of Home's eight headlines carry one: H1 *whole* · H4 *produced* · H5 *all* · H6 *actually*. H3, H7, H9 and H10 carry none; four italics on one page is the ceiling, not the target.

**Radius and gutter, one number (X-33).** The mark's cross-shaped gutter measures 8–10 % of one cell (`_index-logo`). Applied to a 12-column grid at the 1440 reference with 48 px outer margins, a 9 % ratio gives a column of ~103 px and a gutter of 9.3 px, rounded to **10 px**. Radius = gutter = **10 px**, on every cell at every size: the H4 card, the form's fields, the set-piece plate's bounding cell. Mosaic-dissolve tiles are 10, 20 and 40 px, so the tile scale traces to the same token (the S-01 risk). Mobile: 4 columns, 20 px margins, the same 10 px gutter.

**The column grid.** Twelve columns under everything; three exposed full-height rules in the rule token at the left margin, the centre and the right margin, which is the mark's own cross at page scale (its vertical arm), with the running head's hairline as the horizontal arm. Mobile exposes the left rule only. Rules carry nothing and are never borders or focus rings.

**Plate ticks (L-10, lifted from HOLD).** A `+` of 8 px, 1 px stroke, in the rule token, at the four corners of each chapter's plate: where the outer rules meet the chapter's top and bottom hairlines. Home carries them at four chapters and the footer, twenty ticks in all, and nowhere else. At every intersection they would be graph paper, which is L23's look and refused.

**Line weights.** Hairline: 1 px in the rule token, deliberately below text contrast. Survey line: **1.5 px in ink** (`#141614` on every surface it appears on in this concept), round caps, no dash, hand-irregular at ±0.75 px amplitude over an ~8 px wavelength, drawn as one SVG path. Blueprint rules at H4: 1 px hairline with labels in muted mono (A-05), because they carry no photograph. Strike-through at H4: 1.5 px ink, the survey line's weight, so survey and construction read as one hand.

### H1 · Hero — LOUD

- **Text** —
  > **Headline** — Your whole marketing footprint, run for you.
  >
  > **Deck** — Every channel handled, month after month — and you see exactly what was done.
  >

- **What he sees** — *The real thing:* one person, photographed from directly above by a camera that is itself in the air, standing on pale dry open ground (a late-summer field cut to stubble, or a stone terrace), at a sun low enough that their shadow runs longer than they are. They are looking straight up at the camera, so a face is present and readable at the top of a foreshortened body. One warm garment; nothing else in the frame is warm (B-42); the shadow is the cool blue-grey a low sun throws on pale ground. Grain captured in camera (B-24). No horizon, no sky, no second person, no object. *The technological layer:* one thin ink line, drawn flat on the picture plane as though the frame were a survey sheet (A-08), hand-irregular (A-20), enclosing the person and the whole of their shadow in one elongated irregular loop with roughly 40 px of clearance, never a circle and never a regular shape (B-20). At the shadow's tip the loop is drawn tight enough that the tip crosses it, and there the line passes **behind** the shadow for about 24 px and in front of the ground everywhere else (B-28). On the loop's upper-left arc, nearest the person, the line stops, and one word sits in the gap in lowercase mono: `footprint`. The line resumes on the other side. The word is line-work, not copy: it is the one place on the site where a word touches a photograph, and it is said so here. Nothing else is on the picture. The photograph is authored down in contrast beneath the line (A-21): black point lifted to about 8 %, highlights held under about 92 %, grain intact. The un-effected anchor is the face. *Its job:* the ring marks this person's footprint, the ground they actually cover including the part behind them they cannot see. On any other photograph it encloses nothing. *Dial:* subtle.
- **Layout** — Single column with a full-bleed ground. The photograph occupies columns 6–12 and the right margin, running off the top, right and bottom edges with no treatment there. Its left edge is a subtractive mosaic dissolve (B-02) at the 10/20/40 px pitch: the front's foot at column 5, its head at column 7, so the front leans along the flight line; tiles nearest the photograph carry its pale ground (A-19), tiles nearest the words carry bone, with a sparse tail of single 10 px units reaching column 4 (A-16). Because the ground in the photograph is nearly bone's own value, the dissolve is almost tonal, and the person and shadow are the only things that visibly disintegrate at nothing. The person stands at about the photograph's lower-left third, well inside the un-dissolved region; the shadow runs bottom-left to top-right toward the far corner, its tip inside the frame. **The words, all on bone, never across the picture (A-07):** the headline splits at its own comma (F2 A): *Your whole marketing footprint,* in three lines at columns 1–5, top at 140 px; *run for you.* on the fourth line stepped two columns right, beginning at column 3, so the reading diagonal runs toward the picture and stops short of the front. The deck under the second half at columns 3–6, two lines. The accent-filled button directly under the deck at column 3 (L-09). The italic line under the button. The rail at bottom-left, column 1, 40 px from the bottom: `(Scroll)` alone, spent once and gone on the first scroll (X-14). Nothing else on the screen. **Mobile, one line:** the words stack above a second, portrait crop of the same frame that runs off left, right and bottom, the person low-left and the shadow rising to top-right with the loop closed inside the crop and the word kept.
- **Type** — Display headline row; deck row; button row; the italic risk-reversal row; running head; the survey word. The italic word is *whole*.
- **Colour** — Bone ground, ink type, muted for the italic line. The photograph's colour lands in one warm garment and one cool shadow, and nowhere else. The accent: `#1F4D3D` filled on the button, label in `#F0EDE6` (8.2 : 1). This is fill one of two.
- **Motion** — The photograph paints first and is the LCP element. Then the line draws once, from the point nearest the words, at constant pen speed over 1,000 ms, no easing, as a stroke-dashoffset on one path; when the pen reaches the gap it stops, the word appears by opacity over 200 ms, and the pen continues from the gap's far side to close the loop at the shadow's tip. This and the H5 scrub are the two named exceptions to the 300 ms ceiling (X-10). Nothing else in the viewport moves; no cursor reaction, no parallax, no scroll binding. Reduced motion: the line is already complete and the word already set. **The single frame to print: the end state, the loop closed and the word in place.**
- **Feeling** — *Technology, clean.* "It measures a person's shadow and tells you nothing in numbers." Nearest: `B16-aerial-photo-children-shadows-circle-overlay.jpg` for the frame, `A15-russian-zine-cloud-field-spread.jpg` for the line.

## 4. Cost and the one risk

**Needs a real shoot.** The hero is an aerial: a drone or a high vantage, straight down, one person on pale open ground, during the roughly forty-minute low-sun window (B-31), shot for two crops (landscape and portrait) with the shadow's tip inside both. A generated aerial lies about shadows, and the shadow is the second subject, so this frame cannot be generated. The same afternoon, second setup: the same field at ground level at dusk for H10 (L-03), panned along the flight line. About's portraits are real by rule and are not this concept's cost.

**Generated.** The flower clip only (Higgsfield, driven from the parent session; no engine reaches it).

**Built.** The dot renderer with a coverage mask for the scrubbed dial; the per-frame occlusion mask for the call-out; the survey line as one SVG path with draw-on and a word gap; the mosaic-dissolve clip-path; the blueprint overlay; the ledger; the instrument layer (index, stripe cells, local time, scroll cue); the plate ticks. All inside the engineering floor; the occlusion mask is the one MEDIUM item.

**PROVISIONAL edit request, restated, not applied.** The storyboard's H1 prose reads "a real person, warm, shot from low against open sky" and its Phase 2 note pulls toward the glyph field. This concept's frame is from directly above with no sky on screen one and no glyph field; if the founder takes it, that prose is edited to match. The TEXT block is untouched either way.

**RISK, once.** Watched-from-above is one reading away from surveillance on a site whose promise is transparency. The face turned up to the camera and the word in the line are what keep it on the right side; a frame where the face does not read, or a line with a figure in it, tips it over, and both are the shoot's first checks.
