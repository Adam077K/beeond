## 1. Why this session exists

Every visual attempt so far ran the same way: agents generated a finished thing, the founder looked, and said no. Three rounds since 2026-08-26 (eleven GPT-Image mockups · the one-run Fable build with 24 renders · eighteen Higgsfield hero renders this morning). A "no" by eye carries no instruction, so the next round guesses again. The founder's own diagnosis, 2026-09-03: *"a couple of attempts to design something I'm looking forward to, without clarifying exactly what I want."*

The missing artefact is not another render. It is **a written spec, in the founder's plain words, that a designer can be measured against** — what the hero looks like at second 0, 3 and 10; what each section says and shows; what moves; what the visitor learns in what order; which asset is real and which is generated. Once that exists, a rejected version is diagnosed line by line ("section 3 misses the spec") instead of discarded whole. That is also the only anti-supersession device that has a chance here: it turns taste into a target.

Said once: no customer signal exists. Every line of the spec is founder thesis, and the site should be put in front of the two warm prospects as a test before it is called done.

## 3. The readback — what the references say you want

Fourteen statements written after opening all 115 files. Strike through what is wrong. Where a statement survives, it becomes a line of the spec.

1. **Sky is the ground.** The single most repeated thing across the set is a real blue sky — behind people, behind flowers, behind the mark. A photograph of sky, not a gradient. *(A02 · A19 · B21 · B12 · C21 · B22 · B05 · C04 · three logo mockups · Muse footer)*
2. **People are shot from below, in sun, one warm garment, looking up or away.** Never at a desk, never a headshot, skin left real. *(B21 · B12 · C21 · C06 · B10 · A16)*
3. **The machine is drawn on top of a real thing and takes that thing's shape** — dots, plus-signs, ASCII characters, stipple. It is never a robot, an orb, a glow, or a dashboard. *(B17 · C02 · A12 · C05 · C08 · L10 · L12 · L21 · LOGO-07)*
4. **Flowers arrive soft** — blurred, motion-smeared, riso-faded, behind glass. Sharp flowers appear only when they are rebuilt from characters. *(B07 · B18 · A20 · A18 · A23 · A12 · L22 poppies)*
5. **Two grounds, both allowed: warm cream and true black.** About 60/40 light, and the switch is a chapter break, not a mood. *(cream: L12 L13 L16 L20 L22 A19 ISO Muse · black: L10 L21 C18 AgentLab band)*
6. **Type: a light editorial serif for the big lines, a plain grotesk for the rest, mono for labels and numbers — and one italic word inside a headline**, which you saved at least five times. *(serif: runcycle Muse ISO Endex Lassie handhold · italic word: Colab Unfold Superside Lumina ISO)*
7. **One colour, once.** The accent lives on the button and nowhere else. Everything else is neutral; colour comes from the photograph. *(L12 orange twice · L21 violet once · A22 lime once)*
8. **Numbers as texture, never as claims.** A local time, "(Scroll)", coordinates, "124 Total Hours", temperature lines drawn on a landscape, a section index. *(L04 · fe82ee6b · A15 · B16 · C11 · A05)*
9. **The page structure you keep saving:** hero → proof strip → the problem, often on dark → how it works, numbered 01–04 → alternating feature rows → one big quote or statement → FAQ → a photo close with one line → a giant wordmark in the footer. *(Muse · Lassie · ISO Meet · Twine · AgentLab · Ada · Speakeasy)*
10. **Section devices you flagged** (the sections-inspo folder): numbered rows with mono ordinals · italic-lead-word captions under three photo cards · floating asset cards at varied depth · an avatar grid around the mark with role labels · two hands meeting at a spark · dot fields around an empty centre · a halftone wedge inside one card · a two-tone headline where a few words change colour.
11. **Motion you saved:** motion-blurred foliage · a particle swirl at the top and the close · a photo dissolving into dots at a seam · a chevron field thinning toward light · one glowing object between two hands. Rare, slow, tied to one subject.
12. **The mark already has a dot-dissolve version and lives on blue and on black-and-white.** *(LOGO-07 · engraved glass · blue card in concrete · white on blue gradient with a hairline 3×3 grid)*
13. **Expensive means what is left out.** The pages you picked have big empty centres, few words, one image per section, and the words never cross the picture. *(L09 · C18 · B09 · L16 · Form & Found)*
14. **Not in your set:** glass as a system, gradient-mesh blobs, tilted dashboards, mascots, logo walls, three-equal-card grids, people at laptops. (The one robot, Acolite, sits in your full-page folder — read as structure, not as look.)

## 4. Three tensions found in the read, with a recommendation each

**A · Colour.** The locked design language uses deep pine green as the accent on bone and black. Pine appears in none of the 115 references. The corpus says **sky blue as a ground colour and one warm accent (gold, orange)** — and the mark's own mockups are on blue. *Recommend:* reopen the accent as a Step 2 question; test a blue-family and a warm accent on the first coded hero, contrast measured not eyeballed. This may be part of why "the whole feeling" has not landed.

**B · Real shoot or generated.** Every concept and the design language need a real photographed person against sky. Your reference faces are real (grain, pores); the one generated face in the set (B21) is the one the agents flagged. Generated people fail first at exactly the scale you like. *Recommend:* decide in Step 2 whether one afternoon's shoot exists (one person, one hand, sky). If not, the hero is built from what generation holds — hands, flowers, sky, the mark — and real faces enter only through the founders' own portraits.

**C · Static mockups keep failing you.** Three rounds of image mockups, three "no"s, no way to say why. Motion is half of what you described. *Recommend:* the versions in Step 4 are built **in code** (Next + GSAP + a dot/ASCII renderer) on a preview URL, hero first, judged on a live page — not as images.

## 6. How the brain reads the page — laws to verify in Step 1 and apply in Steps 3–4

Hypotheses, not evidence, until the sourcer returns them with a source. Beyond the twelve already in the storyboard (Hick · Miller · cognitive load · serial position · von Restorff · Jakob · progressive disclosure · peak-end · aesthetic-usability · Doherty · Zeigarnik · Fitts):

| Law / effect | What it says | Where it lands on this site |
|---|---|---|
| The 50 ms judgement | Visual appeal is decided before anything is read | The hero's photograph and emptiness do the first job; the words do the second |
| Picture superiority | Pictures are remembered; sentences are not | Every section carries its meaning in an image or structure first |
| Operational transparency (Buell) | People value a service more when they can see the work being done | The site's whole thesis; the "monthly record" and "what done means" sections, and the labour shown in the set-piece |
| Labour illusion | Visible effort raises perceived value, even when slower | The audit is prepared by a person, not generated on submit — say it |
| Processing fluency | Easy to read feels true | Short lines, one idea per section, plain words, no coined terms |
| Gestalt: closure, continuity, proximity, common region | The eye completes shapes and groups by nearness | Dot and glyph fields read as a subject through closure; grouping by gutter only, no borders |
| Peripheral motion capture | Movement at the edge of vision pulls attention | One moving thing per screen, never beside the headline; nothing loops |
| F- and Z-patterns, fixation order | Eyes land top-left, cross, drop | Headline top-left, button on the reading path, image on the off-path side |
| Banner blindness | Anything shaped like an ad is skipped | No strips, badges, pills, or boxed promos |
| Curiosity gap · narrative transport | An open question and a story keep the scroll going | The journey is a sequence of beliefs; each section ends one screen short of the next answer |
| Goal-gradient | Effort rises as the goal nears | The two-step audit form shows step two before step one is done |
| Anchoring with no price | The mind anchors on whatever number it sees first | No numbers at all, so the anchor is the free audit's value |
| Social proof substitutes | With no clients, named humans + shown method + shown process carry trust | Yarden named on Home; the standard shown catching one thing; the phases |
| Choice architecture | One ask, one label | Already decided (FOUNDER 2026-09-02) |
| Vestibular / reduced motion | Scroll-linked motion makes some people sick | Every motion has a still substitute; the set-piece is below the fold |
| Line length and reading speed | 45–75 characters per line | Body text near 65 characters, prose only in quiet sections |

## 7. The twelve questions of Step 2 (preview — asked one at a time, each with a recommendation)

1. **The person on the other side**, described in plain words, not a segment — what they run, what they fear, what they have tried. (Within the ICP-open rule: a description, never a named vertical.)
2. **The one feeling** at the fold, and the feeling at the end of the page. One sentence each.
3. **The hero at 0 s, 3 s, 10 s.** Ground light or dark. A person, a hand, or no human. What the technological layer's job is. What moves, once.
4. **The headline and the line under it** — start from the storyboard's H1 and its two alternates; write yours.
5. **The journey** — the five to seven beliefs, in order, that the visitor should hold by the close.
6. **Sections** — keep, cut, merge, reorder from the storyboard's eight; which three are loud.
7. **Showing work with no clients** — which device carries it: the audit as a real sample, the standard catching one line, the monthly record as a real document, the process, the founders.
8. **The visual system's budget** — which mark (dots, glyphs, stipple), where the bee appears and how legible, where flowers and sky appear, ink, numbers-as-texture. Once, twice, or everywhere, per element.
9. **Motion** — a number from 1 (quiet) to 5 (immersive); what moves in the hero; one set-piece or several; what the reduced-motion site looks like.
10. **Layout and grid** — full-bleed statements vs contained; margins; hairline plates and ticks or none; how mobile is cut down.
11. **Assets** — a real shoot yes or no; what Higgsfield generates; what comes from free libraries; the founders' portraits.
12. **The ask** — the free footprint audit: the label, what the visitor gets in kind, the form fields, what happens in the minute after submitting.

Plus the colour question from §4A, asked as the thirteenth.
