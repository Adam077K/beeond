## 2. Per-image notes

### B01-dithered-dot-pixel-album-cover-portrait.jpg
**Frame.** Square cover, bone ground. Left: a rounded form in colour-sampled dots. Right: a photograph of a bearded person in a white robe and sunglasses indoors, with white squares punched out. Mono caption twice, top-left and bottom-left.
**Technique.** Two different treatments in two panels, not one image split. Left is **conversion** — dots sample colour by position (warm brown high, blue-grey mid, orange low). Right is **subtractive knockout** — square tiles of ground removed from the photo, densest around the figure. Hard vertical seam between them.
**Ideas.** → B-01, B-02, B-03

### B02-social-app-ui-mockup-grid-gibberish-text.jpg
**Frame.** Nine rounded social-media UI cards in a 3×3 grid on pale grey. White, black, green, purple, yellow fills. Headlines are real English with letters transposed: "Social-Media Madia Automation", "save monalx".
**Technique.** Flat vector UI cards; scrambled letter-substitution copy. `DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md` L151 already argues against this by name as "exactly the wrong joke for a company whose central claim is that it does not ship generic AI output." I agree and am not reopening it. The card grid is also a bento, on the anti-slop list.
**Ideas.** → B-04 only. The one non-UI element in the frame is a hand placing a small square tile, and that survives the card grid it sits in.

### B03-dot-letterform-photo-texture-mountain-poster.jpg
**Frame.** Split poster. Left: summit view, blue sky, green valley, foliage at the edges, with stacked letterforms in white dots over it. Right: the same letterforms on a warm grey ground, each dot filled with photograph sampled at its own height — blue at top, grey mid, dark green low. Four words at four positions: "Sometimes" · "is" · "all" · "you need."
**Technique.** The same letterform in both colour modes, one per panel. Overlay left, conversion right. The right panel's dots read as a vertical core sample of the landscape.
**Ideas.** → B-05, B-06, B-07

### B04-blurred-cloud-code-text-openai-codex-graphic.jpg
**Frame.** Flat sky-blue ground, soft iridescent pink-lavender-orange blooms, monospace code fragments in white broken into vertical columns, "Codex" and "OpenAI" in white grotesk.
**Technique.** Gaussian blooms plus mono text as ambient texture. The blooms are gradient mesh, an anti-slop tell, and the honest version of them is real out-of-focus highlights in a photograph (see B07 → B-13). This is also the `779f1ded` note-versus-image mismatch recorded at `ART-DIRECTION-BRIEF` §10.2 — the note describes a white website with cards; this is key art.
**Ideas.** → B-08. The blooms yield nothing; only the broken mono columns do, and only on a ground, because text never sits on a photograph.

### B05-halftone-dot-blue-sky-clouds-texture.jpg
**Frame.** Clean vector halftone of clouds on flat blue. Dot radius tracks brightness. No grain, no photograph, no type.
**Technique.** Uniform radius-modulated halftone. `ART-DIRECTION-BRIEF` §2 names this as the one reference of the seven that **fails** the law, and Layer 3 still cites it as a source for moment 2's mechanism. Both are right: the mechanism is sound, the application is decoration. The field thins at the top and bottom edges, which is the only compositional lesson in it.
**Ideas.** → B-09. Otherwise calibration: a clean subject under a clean grid is the weakest result available.

### B06-blue-dither-halftone-abstract-grain-texture.jpg
**Frame.** Square. Deep cobalt with white dither dots over a mottled cloudy field, heavy paper grain, print artefacts. Subject unidentifiable — clouds or foliage.
**Technique.** Coarse dither on visible paper. Reads as a real print, not a filter, because the grain sits under the dots and the registration is imperfect.
**Ideas.** → B-10, B-11

### B07-blurred-bokeh-yellow-daisies-macro.jpg
**Frame.** Yellow-and-white daisies, extremely defocused, blue sky between them. Warm gold on cobalt. Nothing sharp anywhere.
**Technique.** Shallow macro defocus. **The index calls this "heavy bokeh/motion blur" — there is no motion streaking, only defocus.** That is the same class of error `ART-DIRECTION-BRIEF` §8 corrected for A12, and it matters because B07-versus-B18 is precisely the distinction that decides what moment 3 is.
**Ideas.** → B-12, B-13, B-14

### B08-ascii-text-portrait-blurred-figure-cumbre.jpg
**Frame.** Tall grey frame. A motion-blurred walking figure in a beige coat and dark trousers, with a dense field of mono characters over it — permutations of "cumbre" ("recumbre", "cumbrecumbre") — inheriting the tone beneath, dissolving to isolated single characters at all four edges.
**Technique.** Overlay glyph field, density tracking luminance, loosely masked to the subject. **The photograph is visibly present beneath, so this is overlay, not conversion** — the index's "rendered entirely as repeated small text" is wrong on the mechanism.
**Ideas.** → B-15, B-16. Also the negative boundary case: this converts a *person* into marks, which §2 of the brainstorm forbids. It is the best available demonstration of why — the figure stops being a person and becomes a texture.

### B09-creation-of-adam-hands-blue-poster.jpg
**Frame.** Tall. Flat grainy cobalt. Two sepia hands enter from the left and right edges at mid-height, fingertips not touching. Three-quarters of the frame is empty blue.
**Technique.** A cut-out subject on a flat grainy colour field, entering from both edges, with the gap as the subject. **This is a crop of a painting, not a photograph** — the texture is canvas craquelure, not skin. It is a source for composition and negative space only, never for "real photography".
**Ideas.** → B-17, B-18

### B10-unfold-academy-brand-moodboard-grid.jpg

**Ideas.** → B-19, B-20

### B11-dot-matrix-eagle-blue-sky-poster.jpg

**Ideas.** → B-21. The scattered sentence is **already proposed** (§17.1); B11 only reinforces it, at five positions descending, so it adds no idea of its own.

## 3. Three strongest, and why

**B-05, the core-sample wordmark.** Moment 7 is currently a hole cut in a photograph. Building the letters from dots that each hold the sky at their own position makes the word *made of* the sky instead of *cut from* it. Highest visual ceiling in the slice, and it re-executes an assigned moment rather than spending a new one.

**B-28, the annotation that passes behind the subject.** Occlusion is the sharpest possible answer to the law: a line that goes behind a stem cannot be dropped on another photograph unchanged. It is what separates §17.2's survey layer from stickers, and §17.2 has already paid for the slot.

**B-32, the ghost twin at H4.** The card's argument is one line becoming another. Drawing the struck line faintly behind the rewrite says that in the site's own mechanism, costs an SVG path, uses the corpus's least-spent combination method, and adds no moment.
