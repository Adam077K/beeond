## The claimed white space — and its own counter-arguments

The source states four areas where it believes Beeond is genuinely differentiated:

1. **Whole footprint under one coordinated swarm.** Every competitor found is either a channel specialist (GEO-only, SEO-only, LinkedIn-only) or an AI-augmented full-service agency at human prices. None credibly offers full-footprint coverage at automation economics.
2. **Bilingual HE+EN AI-native delivery.** Israeli agencies aren't AI-native; AI-native agencies aren't bilingual. The source frames this as a real, defensible gap.
3. **GEO baked into a managed footprint** — not a $499/mo dashboard the client must operate themselves, not a standalone GEO retainer.
4. **"Automation does the volume, humans do the taste"** — an answer to the "AI output looks generic" fear that pure-tool players (NoimosAI, Profound) structurally cannot make, because they are self-serve.

The source is explicit that this white space is **not settled**, and states its own counter-arguments honestly:

1. **"AI-native agency" as a phrase is crowded.** GrowthSpree, NoimosAI, Mersel, and Omnius all already claim it. The words are table stakes; only demonstrable swarm orchestration plus integrated delivery would actually prove it.
2. **GEO/AEO-as-a-service is commoditizing** — 9–12 agencies plus multiple $29–$499/mo tools already compete here. Not a wedge on its own.
3. **Speed and price alone is copyable** — defensible only when fused with whole-footprint integration and proprietary swarm technology.
4. **No proof or track record yet.** Incumbents like SAGE (AI21 Labs, Bria AI as clients) and Inter-Dev (since 2007) carry credibility Beeond has not yet earned. "AI-native" as a claim cannot substitute for case studies in an early sale.

Read together: the white space is a real, evidence-backed hypothesis about where a gap exists — not a validated claim that Beeond will win it. Deeper moat reasoning (why this gap, if real, compounds over time) is in `docs/01-foundation/FOUNDING_BRIEF.md` §8, condensed into file 01 of this handoff — `MOAT.md` and `POSITIONING.md` in the old repo (see "What was empty" below) never received that content.

## Substitute-cost anchoring: the method, not the numbers

A prospective buyer evaluating Beeond compares it against four substitute categories. The source's own research grouped competitors into these categories — that grouping is a sound METHOD for cost anchoring and is kept. The dollar figures the prior research attached to each category, however, were marked "(estimate)" in the source — invented, not sourced — and are cut per the founder's strip rule:

1. **In-house hire** — hiring a marketer or small in-house team to run the footprint directly.
2. **Traditional agency retainer** — a human-led agency (Band 1's premium end, e.g. Refine Labs / Tuff Growth at ~$10,000–$20,000+/mo, or the Israel cluster in Band 4).
3. **Point tools** — subscribing to specialist software instead of buying a managed service (e.g. Band 3's AI-visibility dashboards, $29–$499/mo).
4. **DIY** — the founder or team runs the footprint themselves with no paid help beyond ad hoc tools.

**What is sourced today:** the Band 1 human-led benchmark (~$10,000–$20,000+/mo, LOW-MED confidence) and the Band 3 tool prices ($29–$499/mo range, MED confidence) are real, cited figures and can anchor categories 2 and 3 directly.

**What is not sourced and must be derived before pricing:** credible ₪ or $ figures for an in-house Israeli marketing hire (category 1) and for a mid-market traditional agency retainer, Israeli or US (category 2, below the Refine Labs/Tuff Growth premium tier). Establishing these with real sources (job boards, salary surveys, agency rate cards) is a prerequisite for Beeond's own pricing decision, which file 04 leaves explicitly open.

## The missing market sizing — stated plainly

**No TAM, SAM, or SOM exists anywhere in the prior repo.** `docs/01-foundation/TARGET_MARKET.md` is an unfilled template — every field down to the market-size table, the ICP, the geographic phasing, and the JTBD statements is still bracketed placeholder text (`_[$X billion]_`, `_[Source name + URL]_`, etc.). This was never attempted, not even with a rough or invented number. It is a real gap in the research, not a rounding error to gloss over.

The ICP itself is also genuinely open. Three candidates remain live, per the founder's decision this session:

- **A — B2B/SaaS** (the segment the competitive research above was scoped against)
- **B — Hebrew-market lead-gen**
- **C — International, English-only**

Market size changes completely depending on which is chosen, so the sizing method below is stated per-candidate rather than as a single number.

### A concrete bottom-up method to close the gap

Do **not** start from a top-down industry report percentage — none of the "AI marketing agency TAM" figures circulating are sourced to anything Beeond can defend. Build bottom-up instead:

**Step 1 — Count the addressable company population per ICP candidate.**
- *Candidate A (B2B/SaaS, Israel + international):* Use IVC Data (ivc-online.com) or Start-Up Nation Central's finder for the Israeli company count by sector/stage; cross-check against Crunchbase or PitchBook cohort filters (funded B2B/SaaS companies, seed through Series B, a specific employee-count band) for the international slice. Filter to companies plausibly running an active marketing function — i.e. past pre-seed, with at least a small team.
- *Candidate B (Hebrew-market lead-gen):* Scope to Israeli SMB/local-service and B2B companies that sell primarily to Hebrew-speaking buyers. Start-Up Nation Central and the Israeli government's business registry (Rasham HaChevarot) are the relevant public sources; this candidate's population is likely far larger than A but lower ARPU per account.
- *Candidate C (international, English-only):* Use Crunchbase/PitchBook cohorts filtered to English-speaking markets (US, UK, EN-Canada, EN-Europe), same funded-B2B/SaaS filters as A, without the Hebrew constraint — the largest raw population of the three, but the most competitive band (all of Band 1 and Band 2 above compete directly here).

**Step 2 — Apply a service-fit filter, not a blanket percentage.** From each population, exclude companies that are clear disqualifiers (pre-revenue with no marketing budget at all; enterprises with an in-house team large enough that a done-for-you swarm is the wrong fit). This produces the SAM population, not a SAM dollar figure yet.

**Step 3 — Derive SAM in dollars from the substitute-cost anchors above, not from a top-down average.** Multiply the filtered company count by a plausible achievable price point bounded by the sourced substitute figures (i.e., somewhere between the Band 3 tool price and the Band 1 human-agency price, since Beeond positions between the two) — once that price point itself is set in file 04.

**Step 4 — Derive SOM from founder delivery capacity, not from a top-down "we'll capture X% of SAM" guess.** With two founders plus an AI swarm, SOM in year one is bounded by how many concurrent client accounts the delivery model can actually run at acceptable quality — a number that has to come from the operating model (file 04), not from market data. Multiply that capacity ceiling by the chosen price point to get a defensible SOM, then sanity-check it is a small single-digit percentage of the SAM computed in Step 3 (if it isn't, the capacity assumption or the price point is wrong).

**Step 5 — Sanity-check against the substitute categories.** If the derived SAM price point sits far outside the range bounded by Band 1 (~$10K–$20K+/mo) and Band 3 ($29–$499/mo), that is a signal the positioning or the segment choice needs revisiting before the number is trusted.

This method should be re-run for whichever ICP candidate the founder ultimately picks — the three will not produce comparable numbers, and picking the ICP is itself a precondition for a trustworthy market-size figure.
